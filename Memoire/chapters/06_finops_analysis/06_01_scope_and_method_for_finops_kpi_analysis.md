# 6.1 — Scope and Method for FinOps KPI Analysis

The analytical question is whether the published products make cost and usage information interpretable for FinOps decisions. Building a dashboard is insufficient evidence by itself: a displayed figure must be tied to a named measure, grain, period, filter, source table and reconciliation rule. This chapter therefore uses the same definition for an indicator when it appears in several views and checks that subject-oriented datamarts agree on shared totals. Section 7.1 reports the local technical checks; this chapter examines what their values can and cannot mean to a business user.

The quantified dashboard observations currently documented come from January 2025 in the frozen DuckDB POC: 164,145 charge lines, EUR 912,000 in billed cost and fourteen published datamarts, eleven consumed by Streamlit. Section 7.1 presents the local validation scope. The generator has a longer synthetic Daily history, and the Belgium working note reports an eighteen-month DEV/PROD billing backfill. Those facts define potential analysis coverage, but local January indicators cannot be combined with cloud monthly indicators until the cloud outputs, executed revision and reconciliation evidence are recorded. An eighteen-month synthetic trend would illustrate the analytical workflow, not the real evolution of the host organization's expenditure.

For each KPI, the analysis records its business purpose, FOCUS input fields, formula, unit, charge-line or aggregate grain, period, exclusions, source datamart and interpretation. `BilledCost` describes the billed amount represented by the source; `ListCost`, `ContractedCost` and `EffectiveCost` express different price perspectives. Arithmetic differences among them are comparisons, not automatically realized savings. In particular, eligibility, charge categories, credits, commitments and scope must be reviewed before labeling a difference as an optimization result.

The dashboard subjects are executive overview, price comparisons, allocation, resource drivers and data quality. An overview should reconcile to the same billed total as the monthly billing datamart. Allocation slices should reconcile to that total after explicit treatment of unknown or unassigned values. Resource rankings identify where to investigate; without utilization, criticality and business context, high cost alone does not identify waste. Data-quality indicators and audit freshness determine whether a business user should trust a period's analysis. The operational and architecture pages explain lineage and status but are not substitutes for validated cost KPIs.

The local POC supports an Inform demonstration and structured investigation of possible Optimize questions. It has not established realized company savings, automated remediation, identity-based filtering or sustained Operate outcomes. The distinction between demonstrated behavior and intended use governs the interpretations in sections 6.2 and 6.3.
