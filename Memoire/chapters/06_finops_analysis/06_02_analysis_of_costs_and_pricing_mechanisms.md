# 6.2 — Analysis of Costs and Pricing Mechanisms

The executive datamart reports EUR 912,000.00 in `BilledCost` for 164,145 charge lines. The corresponding `ListCost` is EUR 1,290,989.95, `ContractedCost` EUR 1,044,652.85 and `EffectiveCost` EUR 1,101,755.75. Section 7.1 summarizes this local POC validation. Billed and effective cost have different FOCUS meanings and therefore should not be expected to coincide simply because both appear in a dashboard. Their divergence requires interpretation against charge categories and the synthetic generator's transformations before it can inform a financial decision.

The list-to-effective difference is EUR 189,234.20, or 14.66% of list cost. This describes a price-metric gap within the synthetic dataset. It is not a saving produced by the platform, nor evidence that the organization could recover this amount. The contracted-to-effective difference is negative EUR 57,102.90. Describing it as positive commitment savings would reverse the observed sign and misrepresent the data. Before using either gap for optimization, the analysis must align charge eligibility, credits, negotiated rates, commitment-covered usage, amortization and aggregation grain. In the present dataset, the safe label is “cost comparison,” not “realized savings.”

The same local datamart allows cost-driver analysis. Virtual Machines account for approximately EUR 195,399.16 and Storage Accounts for EUR 176,985.27. Together, their unrounded source values represent EUR 372,384.42, or 40.83% of the month's billed total. This concentration gives an analyst a sensible starting point for investigation, but does not establish that these services are overprovisioned or that their cost can be reduced. Utilization, commitments, workload criticality and owner context would be needed for an actionable recommendation.

These results demonstrate that the published subjects can reconcile common measures and expose nontrivial economic questions. They do not validate a company-specific rate policy, a cross-month trend or a monetary benefit. Extending the analysis to the eighteen cloud billing months requires datamart outputs and their Databricks run evidence, rather than extrapolation from January's local POC.
