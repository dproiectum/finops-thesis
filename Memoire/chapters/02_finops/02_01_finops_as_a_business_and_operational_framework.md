# 2.1 — FinOps as a Business and Operational Framework

FinOps provides the business framework for interpreting the project's cost data. A pipeline can reconcile charges technically while leaving allocation, ownership or business meaning unresolved. The platform therefore supplies data for financial questions as well as for reporting.

The [FinOps Foundation framework](https://www.finops.org/framework/) defines FinOps through collaboration between engineering, finance and business functions, timely data and financial accountability. The project applies this framework to a bounded problem: preparing governed cost products and examining what decisions those products can support. It does not evaluate the maturity of the organization's full FinOps practice.

## Why cloud economics require FinOps

Cloud expenditure varies with usage, configuration, location and contractual terms. Resource operators, budget owners and invoice reviewers may therefore need different views of the same charge. Shared cost definitions and explicit allocation rules allow these views to be compared without treating every difference as waste or saving.

## Roles in the proposed operating model

- The Data Platform function provides ingestion, transformation, quality, security and operational controls.
- FinOps owns cost semantics, allocation rules, financial indicators and business validation.
- The Power BI or dashboard function owns presentation and semantic-model standards.
- Managers and application owners consume information for their authorized perimeter.
- Authorized analysts may need direct access to governed datamarts for additional analysis.

This is a design allocation of responsibility. The existing dashboard's actual permission and change processes are described from the student's account in section 1.2; the proposed roles require organizational validation before they can be reported as established practice.
