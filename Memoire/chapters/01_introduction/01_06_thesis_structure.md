# 1.6 — Thesis Structure

Chapter 2 defines the FinOps and cloud-cost concepts used in the analysis. Chapter 3 explains the synthetic dataset, monthly billing simulation and evaluation method. Chapter 4 presents the logical and physical architecture, technology choices and analytical data model. Chapter 5 follows the three implementation workstreams: the independent FOCUS generator, the local DuckDB/Streamlit proof of concept, and the Databricks cloud platform. This ordering explains the final system; it is not a chronology of development. Chapter 6 interprets the available cost and allocation results. Chapter 7 assesses local validation and the evidence currently available for Databricks, including the limits of a performance comparison.

Chapter 8 examines build and operating costs and applies FinOps reasoning to the platform's own workloads, including the later workspace and compute decisions. Chapter 9 discusses the findings, limitations and organizational options. Chapter 10 returns to the research question and identifies work that depends on further evidence. Throughout the thesis, observed results are separated from reported execution and proposed tests.
