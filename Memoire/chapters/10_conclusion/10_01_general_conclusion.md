# 10.1 — General Conclusion

This thesis asks how a FinOps data platform can transform FOCUS cost-and-usage records into reliable products and provide governed analysis for decision support. The project has produced an independent synthetic-data generator, a local DuckDB and Streamlit prototype, and a Databricks/GCP implementation with separate DEV and PROD processing paths. The available evidence supports different conclusions for each research question.

For reliability, the local validation confirms a reproducible source history and reconciled January 2025 analytical outputs. The validated Daily corpus contains 608 files through August 2026; the unchanged monthly billing corpus contains eighteen months through June 2026. The versioned contract, audit model and month-replacement logic specify how the cloud platform should preserve these relationships. Historical DEV and PROD processing is reported in the Belgium plan, but complete run-level artifacts are still needed to establish the cloud result independently. The full Daily-to-monthly-close cycle has not yet been validated in the thesis evidence package.

For governed self-service, the local application executes against published datamarts, which demonstrates a functioning analytical interface in that environment. It does not establish that managers or analysts can use the cloud application within verified access boundaries. Identity-based filtering, direct SQL permissions and task completion require separate tests. The reported professional problem also concerns the time taken to change an existing dashboard; that process has not been measured.

For FinOps decision support, the local datamarts expose cost perspectives, service concentration and allocation coverage. These figures come from synthetic data. The high share of `Unknown` cost-center allocation shows that a reconciled charge can still be weak evidence for accountability. Cost differences cannot be called savings without validated charge semantics and a comparable baseline. The platform's own operating cost is likewise defined methodologically but has not been quantified from a complete Databricks and GCP bill.

The project therefore establishes an auditable engineering and evaluation basis for FinOps analysis, with a locally tested subset of its intended capabilities. Further cloud, access and business validation determines how far the same design can support governed organizational use.
